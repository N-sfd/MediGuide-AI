Tuberculosis (TB): Patient Education Overview

What tuberculosis is

Tuberculosis (TB) is a disease caused by bacteria that most often
affects the lungs, though it can affect other parts of the body such
as the kidneys, spine, or brain. TB spreads through the air when a
person with active TB disease in the lungs or throat coughs,
speaks, or sings, releasing tiny droplets that another person can
breathe in.

Latent TB infection versus TB disease

Not everyone infected with TB bacteria becomes sick. In latent TB
infection, the bacteria live in the body without causing symptoms,
and the person cannot spread TB to others. In TB disease, the
bacteria are active, the person has symptoms, and TB disease in the
lungs or throat can be spread to others. Both latent infection and
active disease can be identified through testing and can be treated.

Common symptoms of TB disease

Symptoms can include a cough lasting three weeks or longer, coughing
up blood or sputum, chest pain, weakness or fatigue, weight loss,
loss of appetite, chills, fever, and night sweats. Symptoms depend on
which part of the body is affected.

Testing

TB testing usually involves a TB skin test or a TB blood test to
check for infection. If a test result is positive, further
evaluation, such as a chest X-ray and sputum tests, is used to
determine whether the person has latent TB infection or TB disease.

Treatment

TB is treated with a course of antibiotics prescribed by a
healthcare provider, typically over several months. It is important
to take all medication exactly as prescribed and for the full length
of treatment, even after symptoms improve, since stopping early can
allow the bacteria to survive and can contribute to drug-resistant
TB.

When to seek care

Anyone with a prolonged cough, unexplained weight loss, night sweats,
or known exposure to a person with active TB disease should contact
a healthcare professional for evaluation and testing. People
diagnosed with TB disease should follow their full treatment plan
under medical supervision to protect their own health and to prevent
spreading TB to others.
