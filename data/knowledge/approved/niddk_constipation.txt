Constipation: Patient Education Overview

What constipation is

Constipation generally means having fewer than three bowel movements
a week, though normal frequency varies from person to person. It can
also involve stools that are hard, dry, or difficult or painful to
pass, or a feeling of incomplete emptying.

Common symptoms

Common symptoms include infrequent bowel movements, straining during
bowel movements, hard or lumpy stools, a sense of blockage, and
needing to use manual maneuvers to pass a stool. Constipation can
also cause bloating and abdominal discomfort.

Common causes

Common causes include a diet low in fiber, inadequate fluid intake,
lack of physical activity, ignoring the urge to have a bowel
movement, certain medications, changes in routine or travel,
pregnancy, and certain medical conditions affecting the digestive
system, nerves, or hormones.

General management

Management often starts with lifestyle changes, including increasing
dietary fiber gradually, drinking enough fluids, getting regular
physical activity, and establishing a consistent bathroom routine.
Over-the-counter options such as fiber supplements or laxatives may
help in some cases, but should be used as directed and are best
discussed with a healthcare provider, especially for regular or
long-term use.

When to seek care

Anyone with constipation lasting more than a few weeks, severe
abdominal pain, blood in the stool, unexplained weight loss, or
constipation alternating with diarrhea should be evaluated by a
qualified healthcare professional, since these can indicate an
underlying condition that needs further evaluation.
