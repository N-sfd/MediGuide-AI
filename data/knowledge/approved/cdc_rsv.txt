Respiratory Syncytial Virus (RSV): Patient Education Overview

What RSV is

Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) is a common virus that causes
infections of the lungs and respiratory tract. It spreads through
respiratory droplets from coughing or sneezing, and through direct
contact, such as touching a contaminated surface and then touching
the face. Most people are infected with RSV at least once by age
two.

Common symptoms

In most people, RSV causes mild, cold-like symptoms such as runny
nose, decreased appetite, coughing, sneezing, fever, and wheezing,
usually appearing in stages rather than all at once. In infants,
symptoms may instead include irritability, decreased activity, and
breathing difficulties.

Who is at higher risk of severe illness

RSV can cause more serious illness, such as bronchiolitis or
pneumonia, in premature infants, young children, older adults, and
people with chronic heart or lung disease or a weakened immune
system. RSV is a leading cause of hospitalization in infants.

Prevention

Preventive options include RSV vaccines for older adults and
pregnant individuals, and RSV antibody products for infants and
young children, as recommended by a healthcare provider. General
protective habits include frequent handwashing, avoiding close
contact with sick individuals, and cleaning frequently touched
surfaces.

General self-care

Most RSV infections in healthy older children and adults are mild
and can be managed at home with rest, fluids, and over-the-counter
medication for fever, taken as directed.

When to seek care

Seek prompt medical care for infants or young children with signs
such as fast or labored breathing, flaring nostrils, ribs that pull
in with each breath, bluish color around the lips, poor feeding, or
unusual lethargy. Older adults or people with chronic conditions who
develop worsening breathing difficulty, high fever, or dehydration
should also seek medical evaluation promptly.
