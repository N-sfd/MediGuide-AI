Melanoma: Patient Education Overview

What melanoma is

Melanoma is a type of skin cancer that develops in melanocytes, the
cells that produce the pigment that gives skin its color. It is less
common than some other types of skin cancer but is more likely to
grow and spread to other parts of the body if not found and treated
early.

Common signs

Melanoma often appears as a new mole or spot on the skin, or as a
change in an existing mole. A helpful guide for checking moles is the
"ABCDE" rule: Asymmetry (one half unlike the other), Border
(irregular or blurred edges), Color (varied shades within the same
mole), Diameter (larger than about a pencil eraser), and Evolving
(changing in size, shape, or color over time).

Risk factors

Risk factors include a history of sunburns, especially blistering
sunburns, excessive ultraviolet (UV) exposure from the sun or
tanning beds, fair skin that burns easily, a large number of moles,
a personal or family history of melanoma, and a weakened immune
system.

Prevention and early detection

Protective habits include seeking shade, wearing protective clothing,
using broad-spectrum sunscreen, and avoiding tanning beds. Regularly
checking skin for new or changing spots, and having a healthcare
provider examine any concerning spots, can help find melanoma early,
when it is generally easier to treat.

General treatment overview

Treatment depends on how thick and advanced the melanoma is and may
include surgical removal, and for more advanced cases, treatments
such as immunotherapy, targeted therapy, radiation therapy, or
chemotherapy.

When to seek care

Anyone who notices a new or changing mole or skin spot, especially
one matching the ABCDE warning signs, should have it evaluated by a
healthcare professional promptly, since early detection significantly
improves treatment outcomes.
