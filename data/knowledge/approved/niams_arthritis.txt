Arthritis: Patient Education Overview

What arthritis is

Arthritis is a general term for conditions that cause joint pain,
swelling, and stiffness. There are more than 100 types of arthritis.
The most common type is osteoarthritis, which occurs when the
protective cartilage that cushions the ends of bones wears down over
time. Other types, such as rheumatoid arthritis and psoriatic
arthritis, are autoimmune or inflammatory conditions in which the
immune system contributes to joint damage.

Common symptoms

Common symptoms across types of arthritis include joint pain,
stiffness (especially after rest or in the morning), swelling,
redness or warmth around a joint, and a reduced range of motion.
Osteoarthritis symptoms often worsen with activity and improve with
rest, while inflammatory types of arthritis often cause more
stiffness after periods of inactivity.

Risk factors

Risk factors vary by type but commonly include increasing age, a
family history of arthritis, previous joint injury, being overweight,
and, for inflammatory types, genetic and immune-related factors.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis typically involves a physical examination, a review of
symptoms and medical history, imaging tests such as X-rays, and
sometimes blood tests to help identify the specific type of
arthritis.

General management

Management depends on the type and severity of arthritis and can
include physical activity and exercise, weight management, physical
or occupational therapy, pain-relief medication, and, for
inflammatory types, medications that target the immune system. In
some cases of advanced joint damage, surgery such as joint
replacement may be considered.

When to seek care

Anyone with persistent joint pain, swelling, or stiffness that
affects daily activities should be evaluated by a qualified
healthcare professional to determine the type of arthritis and an
appropriate management plan.
