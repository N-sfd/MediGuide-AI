Prostate Cancer: Patient Education Overview

What prostate cancer is

Prostate cancer occurs when cells in the prostate, a small gland in
men that produces part of the fluid in semen, grow out of control.
It is one of the most common cancers in men and often grows slowly,
though some forms can be more aggressive.

Common symptoms

Early prostate cancer often causes no symptoms. When symptoms occur,
they can include difficulty urinating, a weak or interrupted urine
stream, frequent urination, especially at night, blood in urine or
semen, or discomfort in the pelvic area. These symptoms can also be
caused by non-cancerous conditions, such as an enlarged prostate.

Risk factors

Risk factors include increasing age, a family history of prostate
cancer, and certain inherited gene changes. Race and ethnicity are
also associated with differences in risk, and prostate cancer is
more common in older men.

Screening

Screening for prostate cancer typically involves a prostate-specific
antigen (PSA) blood test and sometimes a digital rectal exam.
Screening decisions involve weighing potential benefits and harms,
so it is best to discuss an appropriate, individualized screening
plan with a healthcare provider.

General treatment overview

Treatment options depend on the stage and aggressiveness of the
cancer and the individual's health and preferences. Options can
include active surveillance, surgery, radiation therapy, hormone
therapy, chemotherapy, or a combination of these approaches.

When to seek care

Anyone with urinary changes, blood in urine or semen, or pelvic
discomfort should contact a healthcare professional for evaluation.
Discuss personal risk factors and an appropriate screening plan with
a qualified healthcare professional.
