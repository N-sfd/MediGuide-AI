CBC Lab Tests Basics

A complete blood count, or CBC, is a common blood test that measures several
parts of the blood. Educational materials often describe red blood cells, white
blood cells, platelets, hemoglobin, and hematocrit.

Hemoglobin is a protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen throughout the
body. A hemoglobin result is usually reported with a unit such as g/dL and a
reference range from the laboratory that performed the test.

White blood cells help the body respond to infection and inflammation. Platelets
help blood clot. Each lab report should be read with its own reference ranges,
because ranges can vary by lab method, age, and sex.

When reviewing a lab report, check names, values, units, dates, and reference
ranges carefully. A value inside or outside a listed range still needs clinical
interpretation.

Questions people often prepare for a clinician include whether results have
changed over time, whether medications or illness could affect the value, and
whether follow-up testing is needed.

This information is educational and is not a diagnosis or personalized lab
interpretation.
