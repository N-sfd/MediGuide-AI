Pancreatic Cancer: Patient Education Overview

What pancreatic cancer is

Pancreatic cancer occurs when cells in the pancreas, an organ behind
the stomach that helps digest food and regulate blood sugar, grow
out of control. It often is not found until it has grown or spread,
because early pancreatic cancer frequently causes few or no
noticeable symptoms.

Common symptoms

Symptoms can include yellowing of the skin or eyes (jaundice), pain
in the upper abdomen or back, unexplained weight loss, loss of
appetite, fatigue, light-colored stools, dark urine, and new-onset
diabetes. These symptoms can also be caused by other, less serious
conditions, so evaluation by a healthcare provider is needed to
determine the cause.

Risk factors

Risk factors include increasing age, smoking, obesity, long-standing
diabetes, chronic pancreatitis, certain inherited gene changes, and
a family history of pancreatic cancer.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis typically involves imaging tests such as a CT scan or MRI,
blood tests, and often a biopsy to confirm the diagnosis and
determine the specific type and stage of cancer.

General treatment overview

Treatment depends on the stage and location of the cancer and the
individual's overall health, and can include surgery, chemotherapy,
radiation therapy, or a combination of these approaches. A care team
works with each patient to determine an appropriate plan.

When to seek care

Anyone with persistent abdominal or back pain, unexplained weight
loss, yellowing of the skin or eyes, or other concerning symptoms
should contact a healthcare professional for evaluation.
