Alzheimer's Disease: Patient Education Overview

What Alzheimer's disease is

Alzheimer's disease is a progressive brain disorder that slowly
destroys memory and thinking skills, and eventually the ability to
carry out simple daily tasks. It is the most common cause of
dementia among older adults. Changes in the brain, including
abnormal protein deposits, gradually damage and destroy brain cells.

Common symptoms

Early symptoms often include difficulty remembering recent
conversations or events, while more distant memories may remain
intact longer. As the disease progresses, symptoms can include
disorientation, confusion about time or place, difficulty with
language, mood and behavior changes, and eventually difficulty
walking, swallowing, and communicating. Symptoms and progression vary
from person to person.

Risk factors

The greatest known risk factor is increasing age, though Alzheimer's
is not a normal part of aging. Other factors associated with risk
include family history, certain genes, and cardiovascular risk
factors such as high blood pressure, diabetes, and physical
inactivity.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis typically involves a review of medical history, cognitive
and neurological tests, and sometimes brain imaging or other tests
to rule out other causes of symptoms and to help determine the
underlying cause of cognitive decline.

General management

There is currently no cure for Alzheimer's disease, but some
treatments can help manage symptoms for a period of time. Care
generally focuses on medication where appropriate, supportive
therapies, a safe living environment, and support for both the
person and their caregivers. A healthcare provider can help develop
a personalized care plan.

When to seek care

Anyone experiencing ongoing memory problems that interfere with
daily life, or noticing such changes in a family member, should
discuss them with a qualified healthcare professional for evaluation,
since early diagnosis allows for better planning and access to
available treatments and support.
