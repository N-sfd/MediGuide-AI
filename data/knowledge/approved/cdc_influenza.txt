Influenza (Flu): Patient Education Overview

What influenza is

Influenza, commonly called the flu, is a contagious respiratory
illness caused by influenza viruses that infect the nose, throat,
and sometimes the lungs. It spreads mainly through droplets released
when an infected person coughs, sneezes, or talks, and it can also
spread by touching a surface with the virus on it and then touching
the face.

Common symptoms

Typical symptoms include fever or feeling feverish, chills, cough,
sore throat, runny or stuffy nose, muscle or body aches, headaches,
and fatigue. Some people, especially children, may also have
vomiting or diarrhea. Flu symptoms usually come on suddenly, which
can help distinguish it from a common cold, which tends to develop
more gradually.

Who is at higher risk of complications

Certain groups have a higher risk of serious flu complications,
including young children, adults 65 and older, pregnant women, and
people with chronic conditions such as asthma, diabetes, or heart
disease. Complications can include pneumonia, worsening of chronic
conditions, and in serious cases, hospitalization.

Prevention

An annual flu vaccine is the primary way to reduce the risk of
getting the flu and of serious complications from it. Other general
protective habits include frequent handwashing, covering coughs and
sneezes, avoiding close contact with sick individuals, and staying
home when sick to avoid spreading the virus to others.

General self-care

For most healthy people, flu symptoms improve with rest, fluids, and
over-the-counter medication for fever and aches, taken as directed.
Antiviral medications may be prescribed by a healthcare provider,
particularly for people at higher risk of complications, and tend to
work best when started early in the illness.

When to seek care

Seek emergency medical care for warning signs such as difficulty
breathing, persistent chest pain or pressure, confusion, severe or
persistent vomiting, or symptoms that improve and then return with
fever and worse cough. Anyone in a higher-risk group who develops flu
symptoms should contact a healthcare professional promptly, since
early treatment can reduce the risk of complications.
