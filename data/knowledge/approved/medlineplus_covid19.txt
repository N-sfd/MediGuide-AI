COVID-19: Patient Education Overview

What COVID-19 is

COVID-19 is a respiratory illness caused by the SARS-CoV-2 virus. It
spreads mainly through respiratory droplets and aerosols released
when an infected person breathes, talks, coughs, or sneezes, and it
can also spread by contact with contaminated surfaces, though this is
less common.

Common symptoms

Symptoms vary widely and can include fever or chills, cough,
shortness of breath, fatigue, muscle or body aches, headache, new
loss of taste or smell, sore throat, congestion or runny nose, nausea
or vomiting, and diarrhea. Some people have no symptoms at all, while
others become seriously ill. Symptoms typically appear within a few
days of exposure.

Who is at higher risk of severe illness

Older adults and people with certain underlying conditions, such as
heart disease, diabetes, lung disease, or a weakened immune system,
have a higher risk of severe illness from COVID-19. Pregnancy can
also increase the risk of complications.

Prevention

Vaccination remains an important tool for reducing the risk of
severe illness. Other protective measures include good ventilation,
staying home when sick, covering coughs and sneezes, frequent
handwashing, and wearing a well-fitting mask in certain higher-risk
settings, especially for people at higher risk of severe illness.

Testing and general care

Testing can help confirm a COVID-19 infection and guide decisions
about isolation and treatment. Most people with mild illness recover
at home with rest, fluids, and over-the-counter medication for fever
and aches. Antiviral treatments may be available for people at higher
risk of severe illness, and they tend to work best when started
early, so prompt contact with a healthcare provider is important for
those individuals.

When to seek care

Seek emergency medical care for warning signs such as trouble
breathing, persistent chest pain or pressure, new confusion,
inability to stay awake, or bluish lips or face. Anyone at higher
risk of severe illness who tests positive or develops symptoms should
contact a healthcare professional promptly to discuss treatment
options.
