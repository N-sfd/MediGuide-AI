Antibiotics Basics

Antibiotics are medicines used for certain bacterial infections. They do not
treat infections caused by viruses, such as most colds and many cases of the flu.

Amoxicillin is an example of an antibiotic used for some bacterial infections.
Educational materials may describe general topics such as how antibiotics are
usually taken, common side effects, and precautions, but they do not replace
prescriber instructions.

Important educational points include taking antibiotics only as prescribed,
not sharing medicines, and not saving leftover antibiotics for later use.
Stopping early or using antibiotics incorrectly can reduce effectiveness and
contribute to antibiotic resistance.

Common questions to prepare for a clinician or pharmacist include whether the
infection is likely bacterial, what side effects to watch for, and whether
other medicines could interact.

This information is educational and is not individualized dosing advice.
