High Blood Pressure: Patient Education Overview

What blood pressure is

Blood pressure is the force of blood pushing against the walls of the
arteries as the heart pumps. Every time the heart beats, it pushes
blood into the arteries, creating pressure. This pressure is necessary
to carry blood, oxygen, and nutrients to the organs and tissues of the
body.

Understanding the two numbers

A blood pressure reading has two numbers, written like a fraction,
for example 120/80 mmHg.

- Systolic pressure (the top number) is the pressure in the arteries
  when the heart beats and pushes blood out.
- Diastolic pressure (the bottom number) is the pressure in the
  arteries when the heart rests between beats.

Both numbers are measured in millimeters of mercury (mmHg).

Blood pressure categories for adults

- Normal: systolic less than 120 mmHg and diastolic less than 80 mmHg.
- Elevated: systolic between 120 and 129 mmHg and diastolic less than
  80 mmHg.
- High blood pressure (hypertension) Stage 1: systolic between 130 and
  139 mmHg, or diastolic between 80 and 89 mmHg.
- High blood pressure (hypertension) Stage 2: systolic 140 mmHg or
  higher, or diastolic 90 mmHg or higher.
- Hypertensive crisis: systolic higher than 180 mmHg and/or diastolic
  higher than 120 mmHg. This requires urgent medical attention.

These categories are general reference ranges. A healthcare provider
determines an individual's target range based on personal health
history.

Why high blood pressure matters

High blood pressure makes the heart work harder than it should and
can damage blood vessel walls over time. If it is not managed, it can
increase the risk of heart disease, stroke, kidney disease, and other
health problems. High blood pressure is sometimes called a "silent"
condition because it often causes no symptoms, which is why routine
checks matter.

Common contributing factors

Factors that can raise blood pressure or increase risk include: a diet
high in sodium, low physical activity, being overweight, tobacco use,
excess alcohol use, high stress levels, older age, and family history
of high blood pressure.

General lifestyle habits associated with healthy blood pressure

- Eating a diet with plenty of fruits, vegetables, and whole grains,
  and limiting sodium.
- Getting regular physical activity as advised by a healthcare
  provider.
- Maintaining a weight that is healthy for the individual.
- Limiting alcohol intake.
- Not smoking or using tobacco products.
- Managing stress.
- Taking medications exactly as prescribed, if a provider has
  prescribed them.

When to seek care

Anyone with a blood pressure reading in the hypertensive crisis range,
or who has symptoms such as severe headache, chest pain, shortness of
breath, vision changes, or difficulty speaking, should seek emergency
medical care immediately. Anyone with concerns about their blood
pressure readings should discuss them with a qualified healthcare
professional, who can recommend an appropriate monitoring and
management plan based on individual health history.
