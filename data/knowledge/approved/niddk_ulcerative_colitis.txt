Ulcerative Colitis: Patient Education Overview

What ulcerative colitis is

Ulcerative colitis is a chronic inflammatory bowel disease that
causes inflammation and sores (ulcers) in the lining of the large
intestine (colon) and rectum. It is a lifelong condition that
typically involves periods of active symptoms (flares) and periods
of remission.

Common symptoms

Common symptoms include diarrhea, often with blood or pus, abdominal
pain and cramping, an urgent need to have a bowel movement, fatigue,
weight loss, and fever during flares. Symptoms and their severity
vary depending on how much of the colon is affected and the level of
inflammation.

Possible contributing factors

The exact cause is not fully understood, but ulcerative colitis is
thought to result from an abnormal immune response in the digestive
tract, combined with genetic and environmental factors. It is not
caused by diet or stress, though these can affect symptoms.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis typically involves a review of symptoms and medical
history, blood and stool tests, and a colonoscopy with biopsy to
examine the colon lining and confirm the diagnosis.

General treatment approaches

Treatment aims to reduce inflammation, relieve symptoms, and
maintain remission. Options include anti-inflammatory medications,
immune-modulating drugs, and biologic therapies. In cases that do not
respond to medication or that involve serious complications, surgery
to remove the colon may be considered. A gastroenterologist can help
develop a personalized long-term treatment plan.

When to seek care

Anyone with persistent diarrhea, blood in the stool, or ongoing
abdominal pain should be evaluated by a qualified healthcare
professional. People with a known diagnosis who develop severe pain,
high fever, or signs of significant blood loss should seek prompt
medical care.
