Diabetes Basics

Diabetes is a chronic condition that affects how the body turns food into energy.
Food is broken down into blood sugar, also called glucose. Insulin helps move
glucose from the blood into cells for energy.

In type 1 diabetes, the body makes little or no insulin. In type 2 diabetes,
the body does not use insulin well or does not make enough insulin. Gestational
diabetes can occur during pregnancy.

Common educational points include monitoring blood sugar as advised, learning
how food and activity affect glucose, taking prescribed medicines as directed,
and knowing warning signs that need urgent care.

Symptoms that may be discussed in diabetes education include increased thirst,
frequent urination, fatigue, and blurred vision. Not everyone has noticeable
symptoms early on.

This information is educational and is not a diagnosis or treatment plan.
Only a healthcare professional can diagnose or manage diabetes.
