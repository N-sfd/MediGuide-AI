Asthma: Patient Education Overview

What asthma is

Asthma is a chronic condition in which the airways become inflamed,
narrowed, and produce extra mucus, making breathing difficult. This
can happen intermittently, often triggered by certain exposures or
activities, and severity varies widely from person to person.

Common symptoms

Common symptoms include shortness of breath, chest tightness, a
whistling or wheezing sound when breathing, and coughing, especially
at night or early morning. Symptoms can range from mild to severe
and may worsen during an "asthma attack" or flare-up.

Common triggers

Triggers vary by person and can include allergens such as pollen,
dust mites, mold, and pet dander, as well as respiratory infections,
cold air, exercise, air pollution, tobacco smoke, strong odors, and
stress. Identifying and managing personal triggers is an important
part of asthma care.

General management

Asthma is often managed with two general types of medication:
long-term control medications taken regularly to reduce airway
inflammation, and quick-relief medications used to ease symptoms
during a flare-up. A healthcare provider can help create a written
asthma action plan describing daily management steps and what to do
if symptoms worsen. Avoiding known triggers and monitoring symptoms,
sometimes with a peak flow meter, can also help manage the
condition.

When to seek care

Seek emergency medical care for severe symptoms such as significant
difficulty breathing or talking, bluish lips or face, or quick-relief
medication that is not helping. Anyone with new or worsening asthma
symptoms, or frequent use of quick-relief medication, should follow
up with a qualified healthcare professional to review their asthma
action plan.
