Schizophrenia: Patient Education Overview

What schizophrenia is

Schizophrenia is a chronic mental health condition that affects how
a person thinks, feels, and perceives reality. It is a serious but
treatable condition, and with appropriate care, many people with
schizophrenia are able to manage their symptoms and lead fulfilling
lives.

Common symptoms

Symptoms are often grouped into categories. Positive symptoms
include hallucinations (such as hearing voices), delusions (strongly
held false beliefs), and disorganized thinking or speech. Negative
symptoms include reduced expression of emotion, decreased motivation,
and social withdrawal. Cognitive symptoms can include difficulty with
attention, memory, or decision-making. Symptoms and their severity
vary significantly from person to person.

Risk factors

Schizophrenia is believed to result from a combination of genetic,
brain chemistry, and environmental factors. Risk factors include a
family history of schizophrenia, certain prenatal or birth
complications, and, in some studies, substance use during
adolescence. Symptoms typically first appear in the late teens to
early thirties.

General treatment approaches

Schizophrenia is generally managed with a combination of
antipsychotic medication, psychotherapy, and coordinated specialty
care that may include family education and support, social skills
training, and support with education or employment. Long-term,
consistent treatment and follow-up care are important parts of
managing the condition effectively.

When to seek care

Anyone experiencing symptoms such as hallucinations, delusions,
disorganized thinking, or significant withdrawal from daily life
should be evaluated by a qualified mental health professional.
Family members noticing these changes in a loved one should also
encourage and help arrange a professional evaluation. If there is an
immediate safety concern, or thoughts of suicide or self-harm, call
or text 988 to reach the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline (in the United
States), or call emergency services.
