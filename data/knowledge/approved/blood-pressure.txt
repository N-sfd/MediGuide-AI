Blood Pressure Basics

High blood pressure, also called hypertension, happens when the force of blood
against artery walls is consistently too high.

Blood pressure measurements include systolic pressure and diastolic pressure.
Systolic pressure is the pressure when the heart beats. Diastolic pressure is
the pressure when the heart rests between beats.

A reading is often written as two numbers, such as 120/80 mmHg. The first number
is systolic. The second number is diastolic.

Blood pressure can change with activity, stress, sleep, medications, and illness.
Only a healthcare professional can interpret an individual reading in context.

General adult categories used in patient education materials include normal,
elevated, and high blood pressure ranges. Exact targets can vary by age and
health history.

Lifestyle habits often discussed with blood pressure education include limiting
sodium, staying active as advised, not smoking, and attending routine checks.

This information is educational and is not a diagnosis or treatment plan.
