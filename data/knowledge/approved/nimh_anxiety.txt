Anxiety Disorders: Patient Education Overview

What anxiety disorders are

Occasional anxiety is a normal part of life, but anxiety disorders
involve excessive fear or worry that is difficult to control,
persists over time, and interferes with daily activities. There are
several types of anxiety disorders, including generalized anxiety
disorder, panic disorder, social anxiety disorder, and specific
phobias.

Common symptoms

Symptoms can include persistent worry or fear, restlessness, feeling
on edge, muscle tension, difficulty concentrating, sleep problems,
and irritability. Panic disorder can involve sudden panic attacks
with symptoms such as a racing heart, sweating, trembling, shortness
of breath, and a feeling of impending doom. Symptoms can also include
physical complaints such as stomach upset or headaches.

Risk factors

Anxiety disorders often result from a combination of genetic,
environmental, and psychological factors. Risk factors include a
family history of anxiety or other mental health conditions, ongoing
stress or trauma, certain personality traits, and some medical
conditions or substances.

General treatment approaches

Anxiety disorders are treatable. Common approaches include
psychotherapy (such as cognitive behavioral therapy), medication, or
a combination of both. Relaxation techniques, regular physical
activity, adequate sleep, and reducing caffeine or stimulant intake
can also help alongside professional treatment. A mental health
professional can help determine the most appropriate treatment plan.

When to seek care

Anyone whose worry or fear is persistent, difficult to control, or
interfering with daily life, work, or relationships should reach out
to a qualified healthcare or mental health professional. If you or
someone you know is having thoughts of suicide or self-harm, seek
help immediately: call or text 988 to reach the Suicide and Crisis
Lifeline (in the United States), or call emergency services.
