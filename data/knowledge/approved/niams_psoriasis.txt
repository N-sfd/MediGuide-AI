Psoriasis: Patient Education Overview

What psoriasis is

Psoriasis is a chronic autoimmune condition that causes skin cells to
build up too quickly, forming thick, scaly patches on the skin. It
happens because the immune system sends faulty signals that speed up
skin cell growth. Psoriasis is a long-term condition that often
involves periods of flares and remission.

Common symptoms

Common symptoms include raised, red patches of skin covered with
silvery-white scales (most common in plaque psoriasis), dry or
cracked skin that may bleed, itching, burning, or soreness, and
thickened or ridged nails. Patches most often appear on the elbows,
knees, scalp, and lower back, but can occur anywhere on the body.
Some people with psoriasis also develop psoriatic arthritis, which
causes joint pain and swelling.

Risk factors

Risk factors include a family history of psoriasis, certain genetic
factors, smoking, obesity, and stress. Flares can be triggered by
factors such as infections, skin injury, certain medications, cold
weather, and stress.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is typically based on a physical examination of the skin,
nails, and scalp, and a review of medical and family history.
Occasionally a skin biopsy is used to confirm the diagnosis or rule
out other conditions.

General treatment approaches

Treatment depends on the severity and type of psoriasis and can
include topical treatments (such as corticosteroid creams),
phototherapy (light therapy), and systemic medications, including
biologic therapies, for more extensive or severe disease. A
dermatologist can help determine the most appropriate treatment plan.

When to seek care

Anyone with persistent skin patches, scaling, or joint pain and
swelling that could suggest psoriatic arthritis should be evaluated
by a qualified healthcare professional for diagnosis and an
appropriate treatment plan.
