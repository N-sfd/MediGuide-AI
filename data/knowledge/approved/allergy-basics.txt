Allergy Basics

Allergies occur when the immune system reacts to a substance that is usually
harmless for most people. Common triggers discussed in patient education include
pollen, dust mites, certain foods, insect stings, and some medicines.

Symptoms can involve the nose, skin, lungs, or digestive system, depending on
the allergy. Mild symptoms may include sneezing or itching. Severe allergic
reactions, including anaphylaxis, are medical emergencies.

Educational materials often encourage people to note triggers, timing of
symptoms, and previous reactions before speaking with a clinician. Allergy
testing and treatment decisions belong with licensed professionals.

Questions people prepare for visits can include how to avoid triggers, whether
antihistamines or other treatments are appropriate, and when emergency care is
needed.

This information is educational and is not a diagnosis or allergy action plan.
