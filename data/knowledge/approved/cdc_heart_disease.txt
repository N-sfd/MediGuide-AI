Heart Disease: Patient Education Overview

What heart disease is

Heart disease is a general term for several conditions that affect
the heart. The most common type is coronary artery disease, in which
the blood vessels that supply the heart become narrowed or blocked,
usually by a buildup of fatty deposits called plaque. This can
reduce blood flow to the heart muscle and can lead to a heart attack.
Other types of heart disease include heart failure, arrhythmias
(irregular heartbeats), and valve disease.

Common symptoms

Symptoms depend on the type of heart disease. Coronary artery
disease can cause chest pain or discomfort (angina), shortness of
breath, or fatigue, especially with physical activity. Heart failure
can cause shortness of breath, swelling in the legs or ankles, and
fatigue. Arrhythmias can cause a fluttering feeling in the chest,
a racing or slow heartbeat, dizziness, or fainting. Some people with
heart disease have no symptoms until a serious event occurs.

Risk factors

Risk factors for heart disease include high blood pressure, high
cholesterol, smoking, diabetes, being overweight, physical
inactivity, an unhealthy diet, excess alcohol use, and a family
history of heart disease. Risk generally increases with age.

General lifestyle habits associated with heart health

- Eating a diet rich in fruits, vegetables, and whole grains, and
  limiting saturated fat, added sugar, and sodium.
- Getting regular physical activity as advised by a healthcare
  provider.
- Maintaining a weight that is healthy for the individual.
- Not smoking or using tobacco products.
- Managing conditions such as high blood pressure, high cholesterol,
  and diabetes as directed by a healthcare provider.
- Limiting alcohol intake and managing stress.

When to seek care

Call emergency services immediately for signs of a possible heart
attack, such as chest pain or pressure, pain spreading to the arm,
back, neck, or jaw, shortness of breath, cold sweat, nausea, or
lightheadedness. Anyone with risk factors for heart disease, or new
or worsening symptoms such as chest discomfort, shortness of breath,
or swelling, should discuss them with a qualified healthcare
professional.
