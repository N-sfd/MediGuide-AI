Headache Symptom Tracking

Headaches are common and have many possible causes. Educational materials often
encourage people to track patterns rather than try to self-diagnose.

Useful details to note before a healthcare visit include when headaches started,
how often they occur, pain location and intensity, associated symptoms such as
nausea or sensitivity to light, triggers, and medicines already tried.

Emergency warning signs discussed in public education materials can include a
sudden severe "thunderclap" headache, headache with fever and stiff neck,
confusion, weakness, trouble speaking, or headache after a head injury. Those
situations need urgent medical care.

Tracking symptoms can help a clinician understand patterns over time. Educational
tools can help organize notes, but they do not diagnose migraine, tension
headache, or other conditions.

This information is educational and is not a diagnosis.
